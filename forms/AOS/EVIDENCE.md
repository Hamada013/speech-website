# Research-to-form mapping

Evidence checked **8 October 2026**. This is a custom clinical documentation form, not a diagnostic validation study. Reference IDs match the form and report.

| Reference | Support used | Implementation / limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [R1 — ASHA, Acquired AOS](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/clinical-topics/acquired-apraxia-of-speech/) | Adult assessment domains and task comparisons | History, connected/repeated speech, oral exam, AMR/SMR and functional communication. Associated nonspeech difficulties are separated from speech findings. |
| [R2 — Allison et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7890226/) · DOI 10.1044/2020_JSLHR-20-00061 | Differential-diagnosis research and its limitations | Multiple descriptive findings plus motor-execution/language considerations; no one-feature or feature-count diagnosis. Adult context is explicit. |
| [R3 — Duffy et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10171845/) · DOI 10.1044/2022_AJSLP-22-00148 | Reliability and validity of the published ASRS-3.5 | Link to the actual research and separate external-assessment field. Independent observations are not labeled/scored as ASRS; no borrowed cutoff or Arabic norms. |
| [R4 — Haley et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23275417/) · DOI 10.1044/1092-4388(2012/12-0161) | Error variability did not differentiate AOS from aphasic phonemic-error profiles in this study | Three attempt records and a descriptive variability observation; no “inconsistent = AOS” inference. |
| [R5 — Haley & Jacks, 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30486684/) · DOI 10.1080/02699206.2018.1550813 | Word-level timing/prosody measures studied in English-language samples | Timing, segmentation and prominence observations; no acoustic classification or cross-language thresholds. |
| [R6 — ASHA, Adult Dysarthria](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/clinical-topics/dysarthria-in-adults/) | Motor-execution and subsystem assessment | Strength/control, voice, breathing and resonance recorded separately; coexistence with AOS remains possible. |
| [R7 — Näsström & Schalling, 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31407612/) · DOI 10.1080/14015439.2019.1650392 | Small pilot of interpreter-supported dysarthria assessment in seven Standard-Arabic speakers | Interpreter/assessor-language fields and documentation of limitations. It is not an Arabic AOS validation study. |
| [R8 — Benafif et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42366587/) · DOI 10.1080/17549507.2026.2693243 | Review of instruments for Arabic adult non-progressive dysarthria; limited availability and psychometric evidence | Explicit adaptation limits plus activity/participation documentation. No claim that these AOS prompts were validated in that review. |
| [R9 — ASHA, Multilingual Services](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/professional-issues/multilingual-service-delivery/) | Language experience and appropriate adaptation | Dialect, premorbid proficiency, language assistance, literacy and exact sample language. Interface language is separate from speech targets. |

All suggested speech stimuli were written for this project. The English and Arabic sets are not designed to be equivalent in syllable count, phonotactic complexity, lexical frequency, syntax, familiarity or stress. Review them in the patient’s language/dialect and edit them before use. No proprietary test items or diagnostic scales were imported.

The only derived quantities are transparent count/time rates and listener word-count proportions. The code does not choose a diagnosis or treatment from them. Clinicians enter the working impression, supporting evidence, assessment limits and individualized plan.
