Voice Assessment

Voice Assessment Guide

A comprehensive voice assessment evaluates multiple domains to accurately identify voice disorders, establish baseline measures, and develop evidence-based therapy plans. It integrates case history, perceptual ratings, acoustic and aerodynamic data, and stimulability testing.

Core Components

  • Case History: Voice complaints, onset/duration, occupational voice use, lifestyle factors, and relevant medical or surgical history.
  • Perceptual Assessment: GRBAS or CAPE-V scales for standardized evaluation of vocal quality.
  • Acoustic Analysis: Fundamental frequency (F0), jitter, shimmer, noise-to-harmonic ratio.
  • Physiological Measures: Maximum phonation time (MPT), s/z ratio, airflow, and subglottal pressure estimations.
  • Oral-Peripheral Exam: Assessment of articulators, laryngeal elevation, and resonance patterns.
  • Stimulability Testing: Trials of therapeutic techniques to gauge treatment potential.

Normative Data

  • Maximum Phonation Time (MPT):
    • Males: 15–25 sec
    • Females: 14–20 sec
    • Children (6–10 yrs): 8–12 sec
  • Speaking Fundamental Frequency (F0):
    • Males: 85–180 Hz
    • Females: 165–255 Hz
    • Children: 250–300 Hz
  • GRBAS Ratings:
    0 = Normal, 1 = Slight, 2 = Moderate, 3 = Severe

Oral-Peripheral Exam

This section evaluates the oral mechanism including lips, tongue, palate, laryngeal elevation, and resonance.

Assessment Instructions:
Adults: Ask the client to perform mouth movements, tongue elevation, smiling, puckering, and open/close jaw. Observe laryngeal elevation during swallowing and phonation. Check resonance during sustained vowels or speech.
Children: Use playful tasks like blowing bubbles, making animal sounds, saying “Aah” to observe oral structures and resonance.

Breathing & Phonation Assessment

This section evaluates respiratory support and phonatory control.

Assessment Instructions:
Adults: Ask the client to take a deep breath and sustain /a/ or read a passage. Observe breath support, phrasing, loudness, and phonation onset.
Children: Use fun tasks: blow bubbles, sustain /m/ or /a/ sounds, count aloud, or repeat words while observing breath and voice control.

Perceptual Voice Evaluation – GRBAS Scale

The GRBAS scale is a standardized perceptual tool used to assess voice quality across five parameters:

  • G – Grade of hoarseness
  • R – Roughness
  • B – Breathiness
  • A – Asthenia (weakness)
  • S – Strain

Each parameter is rated on a 0–3 scale (0 = normal, 3 = severe).
