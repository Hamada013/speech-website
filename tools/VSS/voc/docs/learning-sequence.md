# Workplace word levels

The original VOC home screen, workplace, games, profiles, and learned-word controls are retained. In the workplace settings, **مستويات الكلمات** opens the preserved word hierarchy. Selecting a level fills its two or three cards automatically and chooses the matching card layout. Each vocabulary category starts with its first level open. A complete, error-free comprehension test covering every word unlocks the next level in that same category. Training, partial tests, and corrected mistakes do not unlock levels. Selecting a level does not award mastery. Expressive responses are not required for unlocking.

## Evidence informing the design

- ASHA, AAC in Early Intervention: start with a few familiar words, favorite toys and personally useful requests, expand gradually, model language in daily routines, and accept AAC without speech prerequisites. https://www.asha.org/practice/early-intervention-provider-support/augmentative-and-alternative-communication-in-early-intervention/
- ASHA, AAC Practice Portal: personalize functional vocabulary; combine core words with meaningful fringe vocabulary and include verbs rather than noun-only banks. https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/professional-issues/augmentative-and-alternative-communication/
- ASHA, Intellectual Disability: intervention should fit individual communication needs, with explicit instruction, modeling, practice, and supports. https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/clinical-topics/intellectual-disability/

The category ordering and batch size are editorial teaching choices, not age norms validated by those sources. This image bank does not supply multiple real-world exemplars per word; success here measures performance with these pictures, not generalized functional mastery.

## Word hierarchy

1. Familiar family and play words, personally useful requests and breaks, drinks/food, social words, body parts, and early everyday actions.
2. Remaining core words, actions and body vocabulary.
3. Play, food, fruit, drinks and vegetables.
4. Family, familiar objects, clothing, furniture and home spaces.
5. Feelings, colors, descriptive words, opposites and senses.
6. Familiar animals, followed by wider animal groups.
7. Garden, playground, transport, weather, outdoor places and the city.
8. School, music, technology and occupations.
9. Spatial relationships and shapes.
10. Letters and numbers.

The 994 available word IDs are divided into 345 sets across the 40 source vocabulary categories and an introductory category, with two or three distinct target words per set. The ten broad teaching themes are retained as editorial grouping. Leftover words are rebalanced so no level contains a single word. Each category has an independent sequence; completion is saved per child, and the predecessor must be completed before its next level opens. The existing training, testing, and optional automatic game flow operate on the populated workplace cards. Hamada keeps unrestricted access and is exempt from learned-word criteria.


Completion can come from the existing workplace comprehension test or a completed “استمع واختر” game using the exact loaded word set. Ear/mouth learned-word mastery remains a separate, stricter criterion requiring all difficulty passes. Editing the loaded cards clears their level association. If rebalancing changes the words of an old set, its prior completion does not qualify the new word set.
